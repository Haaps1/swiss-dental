The Science, Simplified

How Do Dental Braces Work?

Braces work by applying continuous, gentle pressure on your teeth over time, slowly shifting them into their correct position. Here's the science in simple terms.

  1. Brackets are bonded to the front (or back) of each tooth.
  2. A thin archwire runs through the brackets, connecting them.
  3. The wire's tension pushes and pulls teeth in a planned direction.
  4. As teeth move, the bone around the roots reshapes to hold them in the new position — this is why treatment happens gradually, not overnight.
  5. Periodic adjustments (tightening the wire, changing aligner trays, etc.) keep the pressure active until your teeth reach the desired alignment.
  6. Once treatment ends, a retainer holds teeth in place while the surrounding bone fully stabilises.

The exact mechanism differs slightly by braces type — fixed wire‑and‑bracket systems move teeth continuously, while clear aligners use a series of custom trays that each shift teeth by a small, calculated amount.

Options For Every Smile

Types Of Dental Braces: How They Work & Benefits

From classic metal to virtually invisible options — we'll help you choose the right braces treatment in Bangalore for your smile and budget.

1. Metal Braces

How they work: Stainless steel brackets are bonded to each tooth and connected with a metal archwire. The wire is tightened at each visit to keep gradually guiding teeth into position.

  • Most affordable option
  • Extremely durable — rarely breaks or chips
  • Works for almost any case, including severe misalignment
  • Fastest visible progress for complex corrections

2. Ceramic Braces

How they work: Function exactly like metal braces — brackets and wire — but the brackets are made of tooth‑coloured or clear ceramic material, blending in with your natural teeth.

  • Much less noticeable than metal
  • Same effectiveness as metal braces for most cases
  • Comfortable, similar bracket design

3. Self‑Ligating Braces

How they work: Instead of elastic ties holding the wire in the bracket, self‑ligating braces use a built‑in clip or door mechanism. This reduces friction, allowing teeth to move more freely with less force.

  • Fewer clinic visits — clips reduce the need for frequent tightening
  • Often faster treatment than traditional braces
  • Easier to clean than tie‑based brackets
  • Generally more comfortable, less friction on gums

4. Clear Aligners (e.g., Invisalign)

How they work: A series of custom‑made, removable clear plastic trays are worn over your teeth. Each tray in the series is slightly different, gradually shifting teeth in stages — you switch to a new tray every 1–2 weeks as advised.

  • Virtually invisible — most discreet option
  • Removable for eating, brushing and flossing
  • No metal brackets or wires — more comfortable
  • Great for adults and working professionals
